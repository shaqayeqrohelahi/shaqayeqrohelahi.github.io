About Me

Research at the Intersection of Mind & Behavior

I am a Cognitive Science graduate passionate about understanding how the human brain processes information, makes decisions, and responds to visual stimuli — particularly in advertising, consumer behavior, and neurophysiological monitoring contexts.

My master's thesis examined the effect of visual metaphors in food advertisements on customer purchase intention, applying rigorous experimental methodology to bridge cognitive theory and marketing practice.

My research extends into EEG-based mental workload classification, where I develop hybrid deep learning models that leverage brain connectivity and graph attention networks for reliable, explainable cognitive state detection — published in IEEE Xplore.

I also have brief industry experience in quality control (fintech), which strengthened my analytical rigor in applied settings.
