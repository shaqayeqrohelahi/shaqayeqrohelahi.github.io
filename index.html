Biography

Research at the Intersection of Law and Cognitive Science

I am an interdisciplinary researcher with a background in Law and Cognitive Science, interested in the cognitive foundations of human reasoning, decision-making, and the interpretation of language and norms. My research interests lie at the intersection of law, cognitive psychology, linguistics, and cognitive neuroscience, with a particular focus on legal reasoning, metaphor, framing, and judgment. I am interested in developing experimental and interdisciplinary approaches to understanding how cognitive processes shape legal and normative reasoning.

My master's thesis examined the effect of visual metaphors in food advertisements on customer purchase intention. Related work includes an IEEE publication on EEG-based mental workload classification using hybrid models that integrate brain connectivity and graph-based learning.
